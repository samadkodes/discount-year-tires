How to Know When Your Tires Need Replacing

Discount Year Tires 5 min read

Tires are the only part of your car that actually touches the road, so their condition decides how well you stop, steer and grip in the wet. The good news is that a worn tire almost always warns you before it fails. Here are the signs worth watching for — and what each one means.

1. The tread is too shallow

Tread is what channels water away so the tire can keep contact with the road. As it wears down, wet-weather grip falls off sharply. Most guidance says to replace a tire at or below 2/32 of an inch of remaining tread.

You can check this in seconds with a penny: insert it into a groove with Lincoln's head pointing down. If you can see the top of his head, the tread is at or below 2/32 in and the tire needs replacing. Our Choosing the Right Tires for Your Vehicle guide covers what to look for when it is time to buy.

Quick check: most modern tires also have tread wear indicators — small raised bars set into the grooves. When the tread wears level with those bars, the tire is finished.

2. Cracks, bulges or blisters in the rubber

Fine cracking in the sidewall is a sign the rubber is perishing, usually from age or sun exposure. A bulge or blister is more urgent: it means the internal structure has been damaged, often after hitting a pothole or kerb, and the tire could fail suddenly. Either one means the tire should be inspected straight away.

3. Vibration or a pull to one side

A steering wheel that shudders at speed, or a car that drifts when you let go of the wheel, can point to a tire problem — though it can also be a balance or alignment issue. Because the causes overlap, it is worth reading our piece on Wheel Alignment: Why It Matters to understand what is actually happening.

4. The tire is simply old

Rubber degrades with time as well as use. Even a tire with plenty of tread left can become unsafe once it is past roughly six years old, and most manufacturers suggest replacement by ten years regardless of appearance. You can read the age from the DOT code on the sidewall — the last four digits are the week and year it was made.

5. Uneven wear across the tire

If one edge is worn far more than the other, or the centre is bald while the shoulders look new, the tire is telling you something is wrong — usually alignment, inflation pressure, or a lack of rotation. Fixing the cause protects your next set. Our guide on How to Increase the Life of Your Tires explains how to get the most miles out of every tire.

6. You are patching the same tire repeatedly

A single, properly done puncture repair in the central tread area is usually fine. But repeated repairs, a puncture in the sidewall, or a large hole all mean the tire has reached the end of its safe life.
